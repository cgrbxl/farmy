# Vision and scope

Farmy is a farmer-controlled digital memory and service ecosystem for farms and agricultural businesses. The wallet is a logical authority and inventory over distributed information, not a requirement to upload everything into one database.

## What Farmy brings together

Farmy combines AI-enabled adaptability, decentralised source governance and wallet-based trust networks. The aim is an ecosystem that can learn how to connect new sources, respect each source’s disclosure decisions and carry assessable evidence of ownership and contributions between participants.

AI is not limited to analysing contents, embeddings or ontologies: a module can be designed to generate and admit interface configurations or isolated adapter code during operation. This plasticity can avoid a host-module update when its existing extension contract supports the new source. It does not remove versioning, testing, admission policy or the need for engineering where new runtime capabilities are required.

Sources remain independently governed. Each owner controls consumption and disclosure while agreeing common participation, usage and accountability rules with others in a data space. Different Wallet authorities can cooperate without becoming one authority or sharing a database.

Wallets can support owner-signed declarations and third-party certifications, confirmations, reviews or markings over exact evidence. Issuers, holders and verifiers may form chains or graphs of referenced contributions. Each verifier decides which issuers and claims it accepts; cryptographic verification does not automatically establish truth, ownership or permission.

See [the three core concepts](core-concepts.md) for flows, limits and ownership across the existing nine families. This is accepted direction; general runtime extension, data-space federation and credential trust networks still need runnable demonstrations.

## Information and interactions

Inputs include farmer documents, email, forms, sensor observations, machinery records, laboratory reports and official certificates. Preserve original evidence and distinguish it from extracted fields, inferred relationships and advice.

Outputs include evidence-linked answers, reports, filtered datasets, aggregates, credentials and controlled APIs. Data-space participation supports both receiving and providing information through ecosystem-specific connectors.

Copilots may combine permitted private information with weather, agronomy, official guidance, markets and other external sources. They may use LLMs, specialist predictive models, deterministic calculations and tools. An LLM is not required for every task.

## Farmer control

Farmers select providers, instances, data scopes, processing destinations and sharing conditions. Delegates receive limited authority. Read, propose, write, export and operate-equipment permissions are distinct.

Control through Farmy does not remove independent access to underlying unencrypted folders or provider accounts. Exported copies cannot reliably be recalled. Processing in provider infrastructure puts that processing environment inside the relevant trust boundary.

## Incremental scope

Six synthetic macOS slices now cover controlled memory, exact-source evidence, source-scoped observations, one cited local-model answer and read-only operational monitoring and controlled exact-document disclosure. The broader document path includes future S3 integration, multiple Knowledge providers, broader model routes and production export adapters. Plastic interfaces, independent data-space participants and signed-claim trust networks will be developed through similarly bounded use cases, not assumed from those foundations.

## Non-goals of baseline v0.1

No claim of legal compliance, certified agronomic advice, production security or compatibility with an external data-space/credential standard. Local demonstrations exist; they do not establish those broader guarantees. The reference stack is recorded in ADR 0002, and each implemented slice declares its tested contracts and limits.
